Your bank balance is a forecast, not a number

5 November 2026 · The ReplyFlow team

The number in your banking app is the least useful figure in your business. It tells you where you are. It says nothing about next Thursday.

Thursday is when the van insurance goes out, rent leaves on the 1st, and the customer who owes you £1,200 has still not paid. The balance looks fine on Wednesday. It doesn't on Friday.

Three things move the line

Money you're owed and expect to land. Bills and regular payments that will leave. Planned money, like a craft fair or a retainer, that hasn't happened yet. Put those on a calendar against today's balance and you get a line, not a number.

If the line goes below zero, you want to know which day. Then you have options. Chase the customer. Move a payment. Ask a supplier for another week. None of that is possible once you're already overdrawn.

Be honest about late customers

A customer who's already late won't magically pay today. In ReplyFlow you choose when to assume they pay: in a week, a fortnight, a month, or not at all. If you pick not at all, the page tells you how much it left out. You can also leave out any single line you don't trust, and put it back later.

Don't count the same money twice

A repeating retainer can show up as planned income and as an invoice. Link them, and once the invoice is issued that month's planned entry drops out of the forecast. The money's counted through the invoice instead.

It's a guide

It starts from the balance you type in, and it says how old that is. It's only as good as what you've recorded. But a rough picture of the next month beats a precise picture of this morning.
