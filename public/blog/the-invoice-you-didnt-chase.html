The invoice you didn't chase

5 November 2026 · The ReplyFlow team

Most small businesses don't have a late-payment problem. They have a visibility problem.

The invoice went out on the 3rd. Nobody replied. A fortnight later you're busy, so you tell yourself you'll look at it on Friday. Friday's a delivery day. By the time it reaches the top of your list it's six weeks old and chasing it feels awkward, because you left it so long.

That awkwardness is what costs money. Not the customer.

Put the list where you'll see it

You don't need a collections process. You need one list, in one place, that answers a single question: who owes me money and how late is it? If that list lives in three spreadsheets and the back of your head, you will chase the loud customers and forget the quiet ones.

The quiet ones are usually the ones who'd pay the same day if they were asked.

Make asking cheap

The second thing that stops people is the wording. Nobody wants to write the message. So write it once, in your own voice, and reuse it. Something short and polite: the invoice number, the amount, the date it was due, and how to pay. No apology and no threat.

In ReplyFlow the Owed and owing page shows every unpaid invoice with the days late, and a button that copies a reminder you can paste into Messenger or an email. That's all it does. It doesn't send anything on your behalf, which is deliberate. A reminder that arrives from you lands differently to one that arrives from a system.

Check the other side too

The same page lists what you owe suppliers. Late bills cost you goodwill and sometimes your terms. If you're waiting on a customer and a supplier is waiting on you, seeing both together tells you which call to make first.

A five-minute habit

Open the list on Monday morning. Copy three reminders. Send them. When a payment lands, record it on the order and the invoice drops off the list. That's the whole routine.
